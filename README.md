# Honest Resolution Rate

**When an AI support vendor says it resolves 90% of conversations, what did it actually count?**

I audited the public resolution claims of ten AI customer support vendors: Parloa, Crescendo, Maven AGI, Replicant, Observe.AI, Assembled, Regal, Bland, Gorgias and Avoca. The answer is that their numbers cannot be compared, and often cannot be checked.

## What the audit found

| Check | Vendors that pass (of 10) |
|---|---|
| Disclose the denominator behind the headline | 1 |
| Tie the headline to whether the customer came back | 0 |
| Say who decides a conversation counts as resolved | 0 |
| State the measurement window | 0 |

**Average disclosure score: 1.05 out of 5.**

Three findings stood out:

1. **Resolution usually means "no human touched it."** That is containment. A customer who gives up, or calls back tomorrow, still counts as resolved.
2. **One vendor, two numbers.** Maven AGI reports Mastermind at 93% of live chat questions *answered* and 68% of support page inquiries *resolved*. The headline uses the first.
3. **Labels drift.** The same figure appears as a "success rate" on one page and a "resolution rate" on another (Replicant, DoorDash); "resolve 60% of calls" and "automate 60% of calls" (Observe.AI).

Every row traces to a public source. Weaker rows are flagged, not hidden. See [`data/claims_audit.csv`](data/claims_audit.csv).

## The proposal: one formula, six disclosures, one label

```
HRR = AI conversations with a confirmed outcome and no same intent repeat contact within 7 days
      ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                all conversations the AI started, including abandoned ones
```

Any published rate carries six disclosures: **denominator, definition of resolved, repeat contact window, who judges, scope and period, AI only or blended.** They fit on one line, like a nutrition label:

```
HRR 64% | n = 41,200 AI conversations | Jul 1 to Sep 30, 2026 | chat and voice, 38% of total volume | 7 day same intent window | judged by LLM, 5% human audit, 94% agreement | AI only
```

Full standard: [`docs/STANDARD.md`](docs/STANDARD.md). Paste ready contract language for buyers: [`docs/RFP_CLAUSE.md`](docs/RFP_CLAUSE.md).

## Run it on your own data

`src/hrr.py` computes containment and HRR side by side from any conversation log and prints the label.

```bash
python src/hrr.py examples/sample_conversations.csv --window-days 7
```

On the included **synthetic** log (1,718 conversations, generated by `src/make_sample.py`):

```
Containment (no transfer):          81.6%
Honest Resolution Rate:             58.5%
Gap:                                23.1 points
  ended in silence, counted as contained: 130
  voided by repeat contact:               169
```

The sample data is invented to show the mechanics. It is not any vendor's performance.

No dependencies beyond the Python 3 standard library.

## Limits

- Public marketing claims only, accessed September 30, 2026. No access to vendor dashboards, contracts or production data.
- "No definition found" means none was found in public pages, not that none exists internally.
- Two figures come from secondary sources (Replicant's DoorDash "resolution" label, Regal's 99.5%). Both are flagged in the data.
- This does not rank vendors. The point is that the numbers are not comparable, not that anyone's number is wrong.

## Prior work

A [Customer Service Manager checklist](https://customerservicemanager.com/how-to-read-an-ai-resolution-rate-before-you-believe-it/) shows one week of data can read as 45% or 93%. [Zendesk](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/ai/workflow-automation/automated-resolution-rate/) and [SQM Group](https://sqmgroup.com/resources/library/blog/calculate-first-call-resolution-rate) define resolution with a follow up condition. This project applies those ideas to ten named vendors' own public claims, and turns them into a label and a contract clause.
